Retaining Walls

A retaining wall is a wall built to support soil on one side while allowing space behind it for drainage. Retaining walls are necessary to prevent soil erosion and provide support for slopes, and they can also be used to create level areas in sloped yards and garden beds.

In addition, retaining walls can be used to create terraces and other raised garden beds. When choosing a retaining wall, it is important to consider the size and weight of the wall, the type of soil, the climate, and the amount of water that will be behind the wall. Retaining walls can be made from various materials, including brick, concrete, stone, or wood.

What Are Retaining Walls, and What Do the Experts Do?

A wall built to hold back water or soil is called a retaining wall. These walls are usually built from brick, concrete, stone, or wood and may be used for commercial and residential applications. Retaining walls are often used to prevent erosion, stabilize slopes, or create usable space in a small yard.

When properly constructed, a retaining wall can last for many years. However, if the wall is not built correctly, it can crack or collapse, causing serious damage to property and potential injuries to people. For this reason, it is important to consult with an expert before building a retaining wall on your own. The expert excavation contractors will be able to help you choose the right materials and design a wall that will stand the test of time.
